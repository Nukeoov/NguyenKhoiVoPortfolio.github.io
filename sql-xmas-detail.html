SQL Analytics | Sales Performance | Customer Behavior

SQL Query 01

Seasonal Revenue, Cost & Profit

Objective

Calculate revenue, units sold, costs, and profit for each complete Christmas season to evaluate overall business performance.

SQL Implementation

Used a CTE to assign each transaction to its Christmas season, excluding the incomplete 2017–2018 period. Applied SUM, GROUP BY, CASE WHEN, EOMONTH, and ROUND to aggregate seasonal results. 

;WITH xmas_sales AS (
    SELECT
        CASE
            WHEN MONTH([date]) = 1
            THEN YEAR([date]) - 1
            ELSE YEAR([date])
        END AS xmas_year,

        CASE
            WHEN MONTH([date]) = 1
            THEN CONCAT(
                YEAR([date]) - 1,
                '-',
                YEAR([date])
            )
            ELSE CONCAT(
                YEAR([date]),
                '-',
                YEAR([date]) + 1
            )
        END AS xmas_season,

        s.*

    FROM dbo.xmas_sales s

    WHERE EOMONTH([date]) <> '2018-01-31'
),

seasonal_sales AS (
    SELECT
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales,
        SUM(quantity) AS quantity,
        SUM(cost) AS cost,
        SUM(profit) AS profit

    FROM xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season
)

SELECT
    xmas_year,
    xmas_season,

    ROUND(sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(quantity / POWER(10.0, 3), 1)
        AS quantity,

    ROUND(cost / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS cost,

    ROUND(profit / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS profit

FROM seasonal_sales
ORDER BY xmas_year;

Figure 1. Seasonal revenue, quantity, cost and profit. Financial values in USD millions.

Key Findings

  • 2021–2022: $7.09M revenue, $1.64M cost, and $5.45M profit.
  • 2019–2020: Highest seasonal revenue at $7.18M.
  • 2020–2021: Lowest revenue among the four complete seasons at $6.95M.
  • Profitability remained around 77% of revenue across the analyzed seasons.

SQL Query 03

Year-over-Year Revenue Growth

Objective

Compare each Christmas season with the previous season to measure revenue growth or decline.

SQL Implementation

Used a CTE to aggregate sales by year and a self-JOIN to match each season with its preceding season. Calculated growth as the percentage change in revenue. 

;WITH s AS (
    SELECT
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season
)

SELECT
    s.xmas_year,
    s.xmas_season,

    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(prev.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales_prev_season,

    ROUND(
        (s.sales - prev.sales) /
        NULLIF(prev.sales, 0),
        3
    ) AS growth_yoy

FROM s

JOIN s prev
    ON s.xmas_year = prev.xmas_year + 1

ORDER BY s.xmas_year;

Figure 2. Year-over-year Christmas season revenue growth.

Key Findings

  • 2019–2020: Revenue increased approximately 1.5%.
  • 2020–2021: Revenue declined approximately 3.2%.
  • 2021–2022: Revenue recovered approximately 1.9%.
  • Sales recovered in the most recent season, but remained slightly below the 2019–2020 peak.

SQL Query 04

Sales Channel Performance

Objective

Determine revenue contribution from In-store, Online, and Christmas Market channels for each Christmas season.

SQL Implementation

Created two CTEs: one for total seasonal revenue and another for revenue by purchase channel. Joined the results to calculate each channel's seasonal revenue contribution. 

;WITH xmas_season_sales AS (
    SELECT
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season
),

s AS (
    SELECT
        xmas_year,
        purchase_type,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        xmas_year,
        purchase_type
)

SELECT
    x.xmas_year,
    x.xmas_season,
    s.purchase_type,

    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(x.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS total_sales,

    ROUND(
        s.sales / NULLIF(x.sales, 0),
        3
    ) AS sales_ratio

FROM s

JOIN xmas_season_sales x
    ON s.xmas_year = x.xmas_year

ORDER BY
    x.xmas_year,
    s.purchase_type;

Figure 3. Channel revenue and seasonal market share.

Key Findings

  • In-store sales contributed approximately 63–67% of seasonal revenue.
  • In 2021–2022, In-store revenue reached $4.76M, or 67.2%.
  • Online revenue accounted for approximately 16–19% across seasons.
  • Christmas Market sales represented approximately 17–18%.

Recommendation: Prioritize store operations while evaluating opportunities to expand online sales and diversify revenue channels.

SQL Query 05

Geographic Revenue Analysis

Objective

Identify the highest-revenue countries and cities across the analyzed dataset.

SQL Implementation

Used GROUP BY and SUM to aggregate sales at country and city levels. Sorted the results to identify major markets. 

-- Revenue by country

SELECT
    country,

    ROUND(
        SUM(total_sales) / POWER(10.0, 6),
        2
    ) AS sales

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY country

ORDER BY sales DESC;


-- Revenue by city

SELECT
    country,
    city,

    ROUND(
        SUM(total_sales) / POWER(10.0, 6),
        2
    ) AS sales

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY
    country,
    city

ORDER BY
    country,
    sales DESC;

Revenue by country.

Revenue by city.

Key Findings

  • Sweden generated $3.78M, the highest cumulative country revenue.
  • Netherlands followed closely at $3.77M.
  • Germany generated $3.70M, and the United Kingdom generated $3.63M.
  • Within Germany, Munich and Berlin each generated about $0.96M.

Recommendation: Use market-level revenue comparisons to guide geographic planning and market prioritization.

SQL Query 06

Country-Level Revenue Growth

Objective

Compare revenue growth across countries in the latest Christmas season to identify expanding and declining markets.

SQL Implementation

Aggregated sales by season and country using a CTE. Used a self-JOIN to compare the latest season with the previous year for the same country. 

;WITH s AS (
    SELECT
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season,
        country,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        xmas_year,
        xmas_season,
        country
)

SELECT
    s.xmas_year,
    s.xmas_season,
    s.country,

    ROUND(
        s.sales / POWER(10.0, 6),
        2
    ) AS sales,

    ROUND(
        prev.sales / POWER(10.0, 6),
        2
    ) AS prev_sales,

    (s.sales - prev.sales) /
    NULLIF(prev.sales, 0)
        AS sales_growth_percentage

FROM s

LEFT JOIN s prev
    ON s.xmas_year = prev.xmas_year + 1
    AND s.country = prev.country

WHERE s.xmas_year = (
    SELECT MAX(xmas_year)
    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales
)

ORDER BY s.sales DESC;

Figure 5. Latest season revenue by country and year-over-year growth.

Key Findings

  • Germany led the latest season with approximately $0.94M revenue.
  • Austria recorded approximately 12.38% revenue growth.
  • United Kingdom revenue increased about 11.75%.
  • Poland increased about 8.09%.
  • France declined 8.37%, while Sweden declined 6.18%.

Recommendation: Investigate revenue weakness in declining markets while reviewing the drivers of growth in Austria and the United Kingdom.

SQL Query 07

Customer & Channel Segmentation

Objective

Evaluate revenue distribution by customer age group, gender, and purchase channel to understand the composition of holiday sales.

SQL Implementation

Used SUM and GROUP BY to calculate total revenue for each customer segment. Divided segment revenue by overall revenue to determine its contribution. 

-- Revenue by age group

SELECT
    customer_age_tange,
    SUM(total_sales) AS sales,

    SUM(total_sales) * 1.0 /
    NULLIF(
        (SELECT SUM(total_sales)
         FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales),
        0
    ) AS sales_proportion

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY customer_age_tange;


-- Revenue by gender

SELECT
    gender,
    SUM(total_sales) AS sales,

    SUM(total_sales) * 1.0 /
    NULLIF(
        (SELECT SUM(total_sales)
         FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales),
        0
    ) AS sales_proportion

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY gender
ORDER BY gender;


-- Revenue by purchase channel

SELECT
    purchase_type,
    SUM(total_sales) AS sales,

    ROUND(
        SUM(total_sales) * 1.0 /
        NULLIF(
            (SELECT SUM(total_sales)
             FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales),
            0
        ),
        3
    ) AS sales_proportion

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY purchase_type
ORDER BY purchase_type;

Revenue by gender.

Revenue by purchase channel.

Key Findings

  • Ages 1–11 accounted for approximately 38.85% of total revenue.
  • Ages 12–17 generated approximately 30.85%.
  • Ages 18+ contributed approximately 30.30%.
  • Female customers contributed 50.57% of revenue, versus 49.43% for males.
  • In-store represented 64.7% of total revenue, compared with Online at 17.8% and Christmas Market at 17.5%.

SQL Query 08

Purchasing & Payment Behavior by Age

Objective

Identify how purchase channels and payment methods differ between customer age groups.

SQL Implementation

Used CTEs to calculate total revenue by age group and revenue by age group combined with each purchase or payment method. Joined these results to calculate within-group proportions. 

-- Purchase channel by age group

;WITH t AS (
    SELECT
        customer_age_tange,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY customer_age_tange
),

s AS (
    SELECT
        customer_age_tange,
        purchase_type,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        customer_age_tange,
        purchase_type
)

SELECT
    s.customer_age_tange,
    s.purchase_type,

    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(t.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS total_sales,

    ROUND(
        s.sales / NULLIF(t.sales, 0),
        3
    ) AS sales_proportion

FROM s

LEFT JOIN t
    ON s.customer_age_tange = t.customer_age_tange

ORDER BY
    s.customer_age_tange,
    s.purchase_type;


-- Payment method by age group

;WITH t AS (
    SELECT
        customer_age_tange,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY customer_age_tange
),

s AS (
    SELECT
        customer_age_tange,
        payment_method,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        customer_age_tange,
        payment_method
)

SELECT
    s.customer_age_tange,
    s.payment_method,

    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(t.sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS total_sales,

    ROUND(
        s.sales / NULLIF(t.sales, 0),
        3
    ) AS sales_proportion

FROM s

LEFT JOIN t
    ON s.customer_age_tange = t.customer_age_tange

ORDER BY
    s.customer_age_tange,
    s.payment_method;

Payment method by age group.

Key Findings

  • Ages 1–11 generated 74.4% of their revenue through In-store purchases.
  • Ages 12–17 generated 58.7% through In-store.

  • Within ages 1–11, Cash accounted for 86.2% of group revenue.

SQL Query 09

Product Category Performance

Objective

Evaluate revenue contribution, average product prices, and profitability across product categories.

SQL Implementation

Used GROUP BY and aggregation functions to calculate category revenue, units sold, average prices, and profit-to-revenue ratios. Extended the analysis to individual products within each category. 

-- Product category analysis

;WITH s AS (
    SELECT
        product_category,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales,
        AVG(unit_price) AS avg_unit_price,
        SUM(profit) AS profit,

        SUM(profit) /
        NULLIF(SUM(total_sales), 0)
            AS profit_ratio

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY product_category
)

SELECT
    product_category,

    ROUND(sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    ROUND(
        sales /
        NULLIF(
            (SELECT SUM(total_sales)
             FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales),
            0
        ),
        3
    ) AS sales_proportion,

    ROUND(avg_unit_price, 2)
        AS avg_unit_price,

    ROUND(profit_ratio, 4)
        AS profit_ratio

FROM s
ORDER BY product_category;


-- Product-level analysis

;WITH s AS (
    SELECT
        product_category,
        product_name,
        SUM(total_sales) AS sales,
        SUM(quantity) AS quantity,
        MIN(unit_price) AS min_unit_price,
        MAX(unit_price) AS max_unit_price,
        AVG(unit_price) AS avg_unit_price,

        SUM(profit) /
        NULLIF(SUM(total_sales), 0)
            AS profit_ratio

    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

    GROUP BY
        product_category,
        product_name
)

SELECT
    product_category,
    product_name,

    ROUND(sales / POWER(10.0, 6), 2)
        AS sales,

    quantity,
    ROUND(min_unit_price, 2) AS min_unit_price,
    ROUND(max_unit_price, 2) AS max_unit_price,
    ROUND(avg_unit_price, 2) AS avg_unit_price,
    ROUND(profit_ratio, 4) AS profit_ratio

FROM s

ORDER BY
    product_category,
    product_name;

Product category summary.

Product-level aggregation.

Key Findings

  • Toys generated approximately $4.46M, representing 15.8% of total revenue.
  • Clothing generated $3.22M, or 11.4%.
  • Accessories generated $2.81M, approximately 9.9%.
  • Books contributed $2.66M, or 9.4%.
  • Category-level profit ratios were relatively consistent, generally around 76.8–77.0%.

Recommendation: Prioritize inventory planning for high-revenue product categories while evaluating category-level demand and profitability.

SQL Query 10

Customer Shopping Time Patterns

Objective

Identify the days and hours with the highest transaction activity to support staffing and operational planning.

SQL Implementation

Used DATEPART-derived weekday and hour fields with COUNT(*) and GROUP BY to analyze shopping activity patterns. 

-- Activity by gender and weekday

SELECT
    gender,
    weekday,
    weekday_name,

    COUNT(*) AS nb_orders

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY
    gender,
    weekday,
    weekday_name

ORDER BY
    gender,
    weekday;


-- Activity by hour

SELECT
    [hour],

    COUNT(*) AS nb_orders

FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales

GROUP BY [hour]

ORDER BY [hour];

Transaction rows by weekday and gender.

Transaction rows by hour.

Key Findings

  • 3 PM: Highest observed activity with 3,131 rows.
  • 6 PM: 3,105 rows, another busy period.
  • 11 AM: 3,070 rows.
  • Female Saturday activity: 2,894 rows; female Monday activity: 2,917 rows.
  • Activity is generally concentrated during daytime and early evening.

Recommendation: Consider higher staffing and checkout capacity during busy shopping hours, particularly the afternoon.
